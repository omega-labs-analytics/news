## Omega Lens

Omega Lens is currently in an early research and development phase.

Seeing how a record organizes itself over time. 
Most analysis asks what individual observations contain. An Omega Scan asks something quieter: how the relationships inside a recorded stream evolve.

The work is experimental. The underlying methodology is still being developed and validated.

An Omega Scan is intended to provide a view of how the observable organization of a recorded stream changes over time. Rather than focusing solely on isolated events, it examines the evolving patterns of relationship within the record itself. It can surface periods in which a stream appears relatively unstructured, periods in which recognizable regularity becomes more pronounced, and shifts in the character or persistence of that regularity as the record unfolds.

Questions it can help explore
When does the character of an evolving stream change?
Does apparent structure persist, disappear, or reorganize?
Are different periods of a record statistically distinguishable from one another?
Does a system exhibit measurable changes in the degree or quality of its observable organization?
Can such changes offer useful starting points for further investigation?
What it does not claim
An Omega Scan does not, by itself, establish causation. It does not predict future events. It does not assign physical or domain-specific meaning to any pattern it surfaces. Those questions require independent study of the system under examination.

The purpose of Omega Lens is therefore deliberately modest: to make changes in observable structure easier to see, measure, and investigate.
